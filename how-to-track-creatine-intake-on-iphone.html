How to Track Creatine Intake on iPhone

Updated October 2026 · By the Brim developer

Short answer. Install a creatine tracker, enter your body weight, and log each dose with one tap. Brim turns the log into a saturation percentage with a target date for full saturation, so you can see whether you are on track; simpler apps such as Creatine Today or iCreatine only count doses and streaks.

Step by step

  1. Enter your weightBrim asks a few questions about your goal and weight, then sets your daily dose from them.
  2. Choose a protocolPick standard (3-5 g per day) or loading (about 0.3 g/kg per day for roughly a week, then maintenance).
  3. Log each dose with one tapTap the log button on the Today screen after you take creatine. Logging is free.
  4. Watch the saturation ringThe ring rises on days you dose and falls on days you skip. Brim Pro adds the forecast with the date you reach full saturation.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best creatine tracker app for iPhone?

It depends on what you want. For plain reminders and streaks, free apps such as Creatine Today or iCreatine are enough. If you want to see an estimate of how saturated your muscles are and when you will be full, Brim and CreaTrack model that. Compare them below.

Does Brim track water or other supplements?

No. Brim tracks creatine only. If you want hydration tracking in the same app, CreaTrack lists it.

Does it connect to Apple Health?

Read-only. Brim reads body weight and workouts to keep your dose accurate; it writes nothing to Health because Health has no creatine type.

Which languages does Brim support?

14 languages.

Do I need an account?

No account or sign-in is required.

How does Brim work out my dose?

From your body weight. The maintenance dose follows the ISSN range of 3-5 g per day (about 0.03 g/kg), banded so that heavier people get more, and the loading dose is about 0.3 g/kg per day, rounded to the nearest 5 g and capped at 30 g.

Is Brim free?

Logging a dose is free. Brim Pro unlocks the saturation forecast, full history and calendar, adaptive reminders, the full article library and the Home Screen widget: $4.99 per month or $29.99 per year, each with a 3-day free trial.
