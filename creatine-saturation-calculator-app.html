Creatine Saturation Calculator for iPhone

Updated October 2026 · By the Brim developer

Short answer. A saturation calculator estimates how full your muscle creatine stores are from your dosing history. Brim does this on a 0-100% scale: a dose closes part of the remaining gap, a skipped day loses part of the elevated amount, and the constants are fitted to the ISSN 2017 position stand. It is an estimate, not a lab test.

Step by step

  1. Set baseline and weightBrim assumes a typical omnivore starts at about 60% of maximum and sizes your dose from your weight.
  2. Log dosesEach logged day moves the number: a full maintenance dose closes about 13% of the remaining gap, a loading dose about 42%.
  3. Skipped daysA skipped day loses about 5% of the part above baseline.
  4. Read the target dateBrim Pro shows the date the model reaches full saturation (98% and above) and a forecast chart.

Frequently asked questions

How does a creatine saturation calculator work?

It replays your doses day by day with simple first-order kinetics: dosing raises saturation toward 100, skipping lowers it toward baseline. Brim's constants are fitted to published ISSN figures.

How accurate is it?

It is a model, not a measurement. It reproduces the published ranges (about 28 days at 3-5 g, about one week with loading, 4-6 weeks washout) but individual muscle stores vary.

Which apps show saturation?

Brim, CreaTrack and Creatine Diary describe a saturation or muscle-level estimate on their store pages; others focus on streaks and reminders.

Can I change the model?

No. The constants are fixed on purpose so the number stays tied to the published research.

Is it free?

Logging is free; the forecast and target date are in Brim Pro ($4.99/month or $29.99/year, 3-day trial).

Does it use AI?

No. It is a deterministic formula.
